S05MIT 6.7980 · Learning in games: algorithms

Align a model by letting it play itself.

RLHF can be read as a game. Five candidate answers compete in pairwise comparisons, and a policy is a mixed strategy over them. Train it the reward-model way (fit Bradley–Terry scores, then take a KL-regularized softmax), or the game way (run a no-regret learner in self-play until its average reaches a Nash equilibrium). Edit the preferences, blend in an AI judge, and compare what each method ships.

Method 1 · reward model + KL

Bradley–Terry RLHF

πβ(y) ∝ πref(y) · exp(s(y) / β)

Fit a score s(y) so that P(y ≻ y′) ≈ σ(s(y) − s(y′)), then solve max E[s] − β·KL(π‖πref). The closed form is one entropy-regularized FTRL step, i.e. MWU with η = 1/β. Def. L5.7 · Alg. L5.3

Method 2 · self-play on the preference game

Nash learning from preferences

payoff M = P − ½  ·  utility g(t) = M x(t)  ·  gap(x̄) = 2·Reg(T)/T

The policy plays against a copy of itself. Each round it observes how every response would fare against its current mix (canonical setup, Def. L4.8). Because M is antisymmetric, the saddle-point gap of the average equals 2·Reg(T)/T exactly Thm. L4.9. Any sublinear-regret learner therefore drives the average to the von Neumann winner.

What each method would ship

“Best reply wins” is the win rate of the strongest single response against the policy: maxy P(y ≻ π). A Nash policy scores exactly 50%, meaning nothing beats it. Higher means the policy can be exploited.

Things to try

Four experiments, one minute each

Break the reward model

Load the cyclic human preset. No score vector can reproduce A ≻ B ≻ C ≻ A, so the BT fit error jumps and a best reply beats πβ more than half the time. Lower β and πβ concentrates further, getting more exploitable. The Nash mix stays balanced across the cycle.

Watch FTL chase its tail

Select FTL. Its last iterate jumps from one pure response to the next as the leader changes, which is fictitious play (Remark L5.1). Its average still converges in this zero-sum game, just without FTL having a worst-case guarantee.

Last iterate vs average

With MWU and the cyclic preset, the last iterate spirals around the equilibrium while the average settles. Increase the KL anchor and the last iterate settles too, at a regularized point pulled toward πref.

Let the judge take over

Push the RLAIF mix toward 100%. The length-biased judge rewards the padded answer D. Watch the “vs human” column as both methods drift toward D. Self-play only optimizes the labels it is given.
